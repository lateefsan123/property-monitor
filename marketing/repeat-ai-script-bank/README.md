# Repeat AI: the content system

Prepared 23 October 2026. This is a researched creative plan and a bank of **200 draft scripts**, not a publishing schedule or a claim that any episode will go viral. Nothing has been filmed, generated, scheduled or posted by this task.

## The central idea

**More room for the work that needs you.**

Repeat AI belongs in a broker's working life: making local content, preparing for viewings, talking to owners and having an evening. The benefit is a configured routine that supports those activities. The broker still brings judgement, handles replies, updates seller information and does the selling.

The recurring fictional broker is Alex. Alex is competent, busy, dryly funny and improving their workflow. The recurring seller is Sara: informed, busy and reasonably tired of vague messages. Either can be played by the same consenting actor using wardrobe and framing. These are invented characters, never presented as customer testimonials. Do not imitate a real agent's face, voice or distinctive performance.

The comedy targets repeated work and familiar situations, not sellers or struggling brokers. Avoid luxury-car fantasy, guaranteed deals, manufactured success screenshots and the claim that the app runs an agency by itself.

## What the research supports—and what it does not

1. **Time and content are a plausible agent motivation.** NAR's July 2025 US-member survey reports 66% primarily adopt technology to save time and 75% use social media. That supports testing the filming/viewing/personal-time stories. It does not establish Dubai behaviour, a Repeat AI time saving or a causal sales uplift. Source: [NAR 2025 Technology Survey executive summary](https://cms.nar.realtor/sites/default/files/2025-09/2025-realtors-technology-survey-report-09-18-2025.pdf).
2. **The agent has a real content-versus-client-time tension.** Zillow's 2025 survey of over 300 agents reports widespread social-tool use and highlights ease of use. This is US-oriented research by a commercial provider, not an independent Dubai study. Use it as supporting direction, not a statistic in our ads. Source: [Zillow agent tools survey](https://www.zillow.com/pro/survey-agent-tools/).
3. **Industry precedent: personality, media and tools can share one story.** SERHANT's own site combines an in-house studio with agent technology. HeyGen's official Ryan Serhant project describes a recurring content production system addressing limited filming time. These are examples of positioning/production, not evidence that copying them works or an endorsement of Repeat. We are not proposing an avatar or a clone. Sources: [SERHANT](https://serhant.com/), [HeyGen project](https://www.heygen.com/heyserhant).
4. **Make the structure repeatable.** TikTok's Creative Codes recommends a hook/body/close structure, sound and platform-native vertical execution. Its storytelling guide explicitly discusses simple narrative formats that can be produced at scale. These are advertiser-oriented recommendations, not proof of organic virality. Our shorter hook timing and recurring characters below are creative hypotheses. Sources: [Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/creative-codes), [storytelling formats guide](https://www.tiktok.com/business/library/Storytelling_Formats_One_Pager_SMB.pdf).
5. **Batch the work, then adapt.** YouTube recommends sustainable cadence and batch filming. Instagram's professional-dashboard Best Practices includes personalised creation/reach guidance. Neither supplies a universal posting recipe for this account. Sources: [YouTube schedule guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979), [Instagram Best Practices](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/).

Research checked on 23 October 2026. This was desk research, not a frame-by-frame audit of competitor reels, interviews with Dubai agents or validation of a winning format. No forecasted reach, conversion or time savings are attached to this bank.

## The mix

| Perspective | Scripts | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Agent | 80 | Filming, viewings, workday choices, ordinary life, colleague sketches |
| Seller | 60 | Relevant messages, repeated introductions, stage mismatch |
| Split POV | 40 | Seller expectation versus the broker's workflow; two sides of the same moment |
| Product / founder | 20 | Brief, accurate explanations of a real control |

Ten collections give us variety inside **six reusable production formats**. Pilot just three formats first: While you're filming, The seller's other WhatsApps, and Two brokers, one problem. Add others when we can make the first three well. Do not mass-render 200 near-identical videos before learning what people respond to.

## Format recipes

### 1. Meanwhile, the broker

Collections: While you're filming; The broker after hours. Agent POV.

- 0–2s: show the interrupted activity immediately: ring light, unfinished tour, cold dinner. Hook is readable without sound.
- 2–7s: one specific friction, in the broker's own spoken words.
- 7–13s: deliberate change: reviewed template/status/cadence/sending setup. One clear, real UI action.
- 13–18s: return to the original activity: finish the take, walk into the viewing, put the phone down.
- 18–25s: short proof insert if not already shown, then an earned closing line and one CTA.

Recognisable device: a camera clap or the phone being placed face down. Vary settings and outcomes. The broker can still get an important reply and answer it; that is a stronger ending than pretending the job disappears.

### 2. The seller's other WhatsApps

Collections: The seller's other WhatsApps; The introduction loop; Wrong status, wrong conversation. Seller POV.

- 0–2s: seller context and a concrete inbox problem.
- 2–7s: show the generic or mismatched message. One example is usually enough; use three only if the rhythm earns it.
- 7–13s: a specific, relevant message appears.
- 13–18s: believable reaction: a question, correction, not-yet, or thanks. A reply is fictional, never a demonstrated conversion result.
- 18–25s: reveal the actual configured workflow behind the better wording, then the closing line.

Recognisable device: two ordinary notification sounds followed by a small silence at the useful message. Do not make every seller reply yes or book a meeting. Rotate indifferent, curious and corrective responses.

### 3. Two brokers, one problem

Collection of the same name. Agent POV, two-character sketch.

- Open mid-conversation with the awkward line. No logo intro.
- A confidently describes a recognisable habit; B asks one practical question.
- A real UI insert shows one useful control.
- A recognises the problem; finish with the punchline, not a lecture.

One actor, two camera positions, neutral different clothing. The competent broker is helpful rather than smug. No app-generated dialogue, negotiation or appointments implied.

### 4. Same moment, two sides

Collections: The follow-up clock; The midnight message. Split POV.

Show the seller's experience and the broker's configuration in alternating shots. Use the seed's generic message as the friction, the better message as the alternative and the reaction as the payoff. Then reveal the cadence/window/interval that supports the change. Seller reaction is a dramatisation. Do not depict a message arriving out of hours after the configured window has already been applied; clearly mark before and after.

### 5. Same broker, two mornings

Collection of the same name. Agent POV split-screen.

Same actor, same room, same initial task; left/right lanes show two workflow choices. No different lifestyle, commission, lead count or fake elapsed-time advantage. The exact productive action is the payoff. Labels: Before / With a configured workflow, not loser / winner. Film both lanes in one batch.

### 6. Show me the setting

Product/founder POV. Question hook, one real action, visible result, one-sentence explanation, end. Prefer 15–25 seconds. Never turn a three-setting explanation into a screenshot lecture. Product-only clips support the stories; they should not take over the feed.

## Signature example: content day

**Hook:** Agent starts a tour: "This is the..." Cut to the same half-finished take three times.

**Tension:** "I keep stopping to type the same seller update."

**Turn:** Show a real template preview, appropriate status and reviewed automation configuration. Label the demo data. "The repeatable follow-ups have a setup now."

**Payoff:** Back to camera: "This is the kitchen." They finish the shot. Brief activity insert showing an actual eligible demo send, if the isolated test supports it; otherwise show the configuration and describe only that.

**Close:** "Keep your voice for the camera. Repeat AI."

This depicts a possible workflow benefit. Do not add "saved three hours", "tripled my content" or guaranteed sends while filming without evidence. Repeat does not create the agent's content in this story.

## Production rules shared by the bank

- 9:16 master, intelligible dialogue, burned-in captions, safe margins checked on the actual platform preview. Target script timings are editing guides, not measured read times; adjust the cut to the delivery.
- Film human situations with a real consenting actor where possible. Use simple original motion/chat graphics for faceless variants. No generated celebrity/agent imitation. No continuous cursor; mobile touch indicators only at meaningful actions.
- One episode, one friction, one feature and one human benefit. Never recite the full feature list.
- Use a small reusable kit: one broker outfit plus one alternate, tripod, phone, desk, ring light, ordinary local B-roll, two dialogue voices if useful, quiet music and a few restrained sound cues. Save a clean source master without platform UI or watermark.
- Fictional conversations and market examples carry a discreet but readable Dramatisation / Demo data label. Do not invent a listing, buyer, transaction or live price to persuade a real seller.
- Verify the current mobile UI at production time. A draft script is not product verification. In particular check saved views, card behaviour, status assignment and actual send state before showing them.
- Statuses and templates need deliberate correct configuration. Do not imply that replies automatically change status, book appointments, trigger arbitrary follow-ups or switch first/second-message text. Broker-card behaviour and message text are distinct controls.
- Configured automation depends on connected WhatsApp, eligibility and sending settings. A queue is not a delivered message. Do not film manual sends to real contacts. Respect a seller's instruction to stop contact; never use interval settings as an account-safety or anti-ban claim.
- Ask Repeat remains a brief supporting research feature, accurately labelled Early access and limited to supported questions. No deal negotiation, guaranteed valuation or all-knowing assistant claims.
- Reuse the same story differently: Reel/Short = full micro-story; Instagram Stories = context, tension, payoff + native poll; WhatsApp Status = concise story or selected excerpt with a conversational caption. Adapt CTA and pacing; do not simply dump three disconnected frames into every channel.

## Repeatable weekly process

1. Choose three episodes across different pilot formats. The browser bank marks 12 starting candidates; they are not a locked posting calendar.
2. Check each product action and claim. Write a small exact capture list; keep any missing-feature blocker visible.
3. Read the dialogue aloud. Remove filler; let the specific situation carry the hook. Film alternative opening lines only when genuinely distinct.
4. Batch actor shots by location/outfit and capture current UI inserts separately. Claude owns the video craft; this task creates scripts, not another film implementation.
5. Cut each story, watch the moving export on a phone, check audio/captions and ask: can a broker recognise the situation before the product reveal?
6. Derive Story/Status adaptations, review the actual files and captions together, then publish only on explicit instruction.
7. Record results at comparable windows (proposed 48 hours and seven days): reach/views, watch/retention where available, saves, shares, profile actions, qualified broker replies and attributable demo/sign-up activity. Platforms expose different metrics; do not compare raw view counts as equivalent.
8. After six to twelve posts, assess a format's pattern rather than one unusually large or small result. Test one change at a time: opening shot, hook wording or proof length. Keep the familiar narrative spine. A higher watch rate without broker interest may be entertaining the wrong audience.

No universal best hour, required hashtag count or magic daily volume is assumed. Start with a batch the team can finish well. Add volume only when production and the audience evidence support it.

## How to use the files

- `index.html`: offline searchable viewer with perspective, collection and starter filters; expanded scripts include shot beats, dialogue, proof instructions, caption and Story adaptation.
- `scripts.json`: all 200 structured drafts, IDs RA-001 through RA-200.
- `SCRIPTS.md`: readable complete export.
- `seeds.txt`: concise authored source dialogue/premises. Five fields per episode: hook, tension, turn, reaction, closing line.
- `build-bank.py`: deterministic local builder and integrity checks. No network calls, social credentials or publishing integrations.

All scripts are draft concepts. The word 'better' in the source distinguishes the proposed relevant message from the fictional generic message; it is not a measured performance claim. The bank is ready for selection and production development, not automatic publishing.
